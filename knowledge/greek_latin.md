# Ancient Greek and Latin scripts

## Greek
The Greek alphabet was adapted from Phoenician around the 8th century BCE, adding vowel letters. Epigraphic hands (inscriptions) use capital forms; papyri and codices use uncial, then minuscule (from the 9th century CE). Major sources: inscriptions, Egyptian papyri (Oxyrhynchus), and Byzantine manuscripts.

## Latin
The Latin alphabet derives from Etruscan and Greek models (7th century BCE onward). Key hands: *capitalis monumentalis* (Roman inscriptions), rustic capitals, uncial, and Carolingian minuscule (c. 800 CE), which is the ancestor of modern lowercase letters. Manuscripts use many abbreviations (scribal contractions) that make transcription difficult.

## Reading notes
Scriptio continua (no word spaces) is common in both. Tesseract has Greek and Latin models but performs poorly on ancient hands; fine-tuned HTR models do better.

Sources: Bischoff, *Latin Palaeography*; Thompson, *An Introduction to Greek and Latin Palaeography*.
