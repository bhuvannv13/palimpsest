# Linear B

## Origins
Linear B is a syllabic script used c. 1450–1200 BCE on Crete (Knossos) and the Greek mainland (Pylos, Mycenae, Thebes) to write an early form of Greek, Mycenaean. It derives from the undeciphered Linear A. Clay tablets record palace administration: livestock, textiles, rations, offerings.

## Decipherment
Arthur Evans discovered the tablets at Knossos from 1900. Alice Kober's analysis of inflection patterns laid groundwork; Michael Ventris showed in 1952 the language was Greek, with John Chadwick developing the interpretation.

## Reading notes
About 87 syllabic signs plus ideograms and numerals. Tablets are administrative lists, not literature. Linear A and Cretan hieroglyphs remain undeciphered.

Sources: Chadwick, *The Decipherment of Linear B* (1958); British Museum / Ashmolean resources on Knossos.
