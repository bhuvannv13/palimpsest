# Runic, Ogham, Oracle-bone and Hebrew writing

## Runic
Runes were used by Germanic peoples from about the 2nd century CE (Elder Futhark, 24 characters), later Anglo-Saxon futhorc and the Younger Futhark of the Viking Age (16 characters). Inscriptions appear on stones, metalwork and wood. Straight strokes suit carving.

## Ogham
Ogham is an early medieval Irish script (c. 4th–6th century CE) of notches and strokes along the edge of a standing stone, writing Primitive Irish. It has about 20 core letters in four groups.

## Oracle-bone script
The earliest well-attested Chinese writing, from the late Shang dynasty (c. 1250–1046 BCE), incised on ox scapulae and turtle plastrons for divination, mostly from Anyang. The bones were recognised as inscribed in 1899; Wang Yirong is traditionally credited.

## Hebrew
Paleo-Hebrew gave way to the Aramaic-derived square script in the Second Temple period. The Dead Sea Scrolls (found from 1947 at Qumran; roughly 3rd century BCE to 1st century CE) include both. Hebrew is written right to left.

Sources: Page, *Runes* (1987); McManus, *A Guide to Ogam* (1991); Keightley, *Sources of Shang History* (1978); Tov, *Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible*.
