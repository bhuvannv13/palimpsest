# Brahmi and Tamil-Brahmi

## Brahmi
Brahmi is the ancestor of most scripts of South and Southeast Asia, including Devanagari. Its best-known early use is the edicts of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE), in Prakrit. James Prinsep deciphered it in the 1830s, building on work by Christian Lassen and others.

## Tamil-Brahmi
Tamil-Brahmi adapts Brahmi to write Tamil and appears on cave inscriptions and pottery. Securely attested from about the 3rd–2nd century BCE; excavations such as Keezhadi have prompted claims of earlier dates, which remain debated among specialists.

## Devanagari and Sanskrit manuscripts
Devanagari developed from Brahmi through Gupta and Nagari forms by the 1st millennium CE. Sanskrit manuscripts are written on palm leaf, birch bark or paper. Ligatures (conjunct consonants) complicate OCR.

Sources: Salomon, *Indian Epigraphy* (1998); Mahadevan, *Early Tamil Epigraphy* (2003).
